Binderscope · user guide

From a finished campaign to a shortlist you can defend.

Binderscope takes the designs a campaign produced, groups them by backbone, checks each one on the full-length target, says which end can carry a tag, and keeps your decisions with their reasons. This guide gets you productive in about ten minutes; the rest is reference.

Binderscope is the program; BindCraft 2 is the design engine it drives. BindCraft is named below wherever it is the thing that acted — it designed the backbones, it chose its own filters, and its scores are carried over as its own. The families, the full-length checks, the paratope and tag analyses, the verdicts and the review are Binderscope's. This guide covers the review: the pages you read once a campaign has finished, whether Binderscope launched it for you or you ran BindCraft yourself and point Binderscope at the run folders.

1Quick start

Binderscope is an app on your own computer. You choose your BindCraft runs and the target, it analyses them, and it opens the review pages in its own window. Everything stays on your machine and your designs are not uploaded.

  1. Install Binderscope (once)

    On a Mac, open the Binderscope folder you downloaded and double-click Install Binderscope.command. A Terminal window shows the progress for a few minutes. It checks Apple's Command Line Tools (it asks you to install them once if they are missing), finds or installs Python, and creates the app Binderscope in the Applications folder of your home folder. It never asks for an administrator password. When it is done, Binderscope opens.

    Not on a Mac, or happier in a terminal? See the command line below. If the installer stops with a message, the app guide explains each one.

    Once. To update, run the installer again.
  2. Open Binderscope

    Press Cmd+Space, type Binderscope and press Return. It opens its own window with the list of your projects, empty at first. Your projects are kept in the folder Binderscope in your home folder. The app closes itself about a minute after you close its window; your projects stay.

  3. Choose your runs

    Click New project. On the first card press Add run folders… and choose one or more BindCraft run folders: the folder of each run, the one that holds the CSV files, the Accepted folder and the settings file. You can also paste a path.

    Each run shows what was found: the BindCraft version, the accepted and rejected designs, the families, the hotspots and the binder lengths. Give each run a short name (run01, run02). Your folders stay where they are and are never changed. Folders you pruned are fine: a missing part switches off the features that need it, and the Overview says which.

    New project · card 1
  4. Add the target

    On the second card choose the structure you gave BindCraft (the page suggests files it found next to your run) and the full-length reference structure of the target: a file, a file dropped on the box, or a PDB id that the page downloads from the RCSB when you press Download. Once both files are chosen the page says how well your structure fits the chain of the reference (a green, amber or red message with the CA RMSD and the residues matched); red usually means the wrong chain, another protein or another numbering.

    The reference supplies everything BindCraft never saw: other subunits, the rest of the chain, glycans. See the target frame and when data are missing. The third card, Options, is closed and optional: project name, UniProt accession (with an opt-in online lookup), the chain of the target in the reference, the other subunits, how designs are placed on the full-length protein and a profile file. If the reference is an AlphaFold model of a protein with several domains, read the warning about how well it places them under when data are missing.

    New project · cards 2 and 3
  5. Build

    Press Build project. The page shows the nine steps of the analysis as they run. The first build takes a minute or two for a typical project and grows with the number of designs; a rebuild with unchanged inputs reuses a cache and takes seconds. When the page says your project is ready, press Open the project.

    If the build stops, the page says why in plain words, for example that a chain is not in the reference file. Fix the choice with Back to the inputs and build again.

    Build page
  6. Read the Overview

    The first page lists your runs with their counts, what stands out in the data (each item links to the page or design it is about), what the project carries and which checks are switched off for lack of data. Read the switched-off list: it tells you what the build could not do with your inputs. A ‹ Projects link at the left of every page's header leads back to your projects.

    Overview
  7. Start on the Board

    Read the funnel and the diversity panel for each run, then walk the tiles with the arrow keys and press K to keep, M for maybe, X to reject (the same key again clears it). Your decisions are saved in this browser as you go, and the app keeps a copy in the project folder.

Where things are

Each project is a folder in Binderscope in your home folder: the analysis in work, the review pages in bundle. In a project's More menu you find Edit the inputs… (change or add a run, then build), Update the review pages (after you update Binderscope), Rebuild, Show in Finder and Delete project. i
Sharing a bundle

Choose More and Show in Finder on the project, zip its bundle folder and send it. Whoever opens index.html sees the same pages and data. It holds your binder sequences, and its build record (data/manifest.json) names your input files and folders (names only: the absolute paths of your machine are cut off) and copies the settings of each run, so check both before it leaves your lab. Decisions travel separately as a small review file (see Saving, sharing and exporting).

Prefer the command line? · advanced use, scripts, other systems

The app runs the same build that the command line offers, so you can script it or use it where the installer does not apply. You need Python 3.12 or newer; pip install -e ".[dev]" in a virtual environment installs the package (the dev extra adds pytest and can be left out unless you run the tests). Then point the command at the run folders (give each an id before the equals sign), the construct and the reference, and open the bundle it writes:

# from the project folder, with its virtual environment active
binder-review import run01=/path/to/run01 run02=/path/to/run02 --target-pdb construct.pdb --reference-cif full_length.cif --name my-target --out work/my-target --bundle bundles/my-target
open bundles/my-target/index.html

binder-review app starts the app from a terminal. Input folders are never written to. Every option and exit code is in the command-line reference.

2Five ideas that make the pages click

Family

One backbone, several sequences

BindCraft designs a backbone (one trajectory), then proposes several sequences for it. Binderscope counts families, never sequences, because sequences of one backbone share a fold, a pose and most of their risk: they are not independent bets. The funnel, the diversity estimate, the tiles and the order list all work on families. You see it as the tiles on the Board, the sequence table in the focus panel and the Siblings tab in the Inspector.

Target frame

The construct is not the protein

BindCraft only sees the piece of the target you gave it. Binderscope puts every design back on the full-length protein, with the other subunits and the rest of the chain taken from your reference structure, so it can ask what the construct hid. A binder that crashes into the part BindCraft never saw gets flagged. You see it as the Target frame strip and the Target checks in the Inspector. BindCraft predicts and relaxes its own copy of the target, which differs a little from your reference, so how a design is placed matters: each design is fitted onto the reference by a superposition weighted around its own interface, which keeps the binder where it sits against the epitope. A fit over the whole target can leave it 1 to 3 Å off where the two copies differ (it is the option Fit the whole target, --placement whole). The interface numbers (contacts, hydrogen bonds, buried area) come from the design's own structure and do not depend on this; the distances to the other subunit, the rest of the protein and the sugars, and the Context verdict, do.

Intended site

Hotspots are the plan

The hotspots in your run settings define the site you meant to hit. A design that touches none of them is marked off-site ("wrong site" in the Inspector), however good its scores. The hotspots come from the settings JSON of the run or, without it, from the hotspot column of the CSVs. A run recorded without any hotspot has no intended site, and the pages say so (see when data are missing).

Runs

Never pool the estimates

Different hotspots, lengths or targets make different experiments. On the Board the funnel, the diversity count and the "Do you need more designs?" estimate are made per run; tiles from all runs can still sit in one list, and the other pages show per-run counts next to the pooled view. Give every run its own name when you add it (run01=... on the command line), and pick one run in the header to see its own numbers.

Screens

Every score is a screen

Checks flag what deserves a look. They are not verdicts, and a design that passes them is a predicted binder, not a measured one. The Inspector's Target checks and Specificity rules say which experiment would confirm or refute each flag.

3The pages

The Overview is the landing page and the header links every page. With more than one run, RUNS in the header picks one run or all, and the choice follows you from page to page. What to try (on the Board, Inspector, Map and Query pages) gives hints for the page you are on. A design id on the Map and Query pages or in the Board's order list opens that design in the Inspector. In the app, a ‹ Projects link at the left of each page's header leads back to your projects. The pages follow your system's light or dark setting; add #dark or #light to a page's address to force one (this guide has its own Theme button). Everything is drawn from files in the bundle: no page needs a network.

Each page below has a table of its features: the feature, the question it answers and how to use it. Order of reading for a first review: Overview, Board, Query, Inspector, Map.

Family board · start here

!
Easy to miss on the Board

Hide rejected is on by default, so a tile you reject disappears at once (the counters still count it). Rejected here means rejected by you; BindCraft's rejected sequences and the verdict NO PASS are different things. With Sort on Learned, tiles you have decided come first and the rest follow by the model's keep chance, so the order changes as it learns; p 98% is the model's guess of your decision, not a binding probability. The number keys tag the focused item if it is decided, otherwise the one you decided last, and tags are not undone by U. Undo works only until the page reloads or you load a review file. The toolbar settings, the cluster slider and the find text are not stored. A link such as board.html#f=<family id> can change the run filter and untick the filters without asking. The verdicts are Binderscope's own checks: a design BindCraft rejected can read PASS, an accepted one can read NO PASS, and an off-site design reads REVIEW, not NO PASS.

+
Decisions belong to families

K, M and X decide for the whole backbone family; the sequences you actually order are chosen with the Order boxes in the focus panel. Switch Unit to Sequences if you prefer to decide sequence by sequence.

Design inspector

One design in depth, with findings linked to the 3D view. Open it with Enter or Inspect on the Board, or from a design id on another page. The address ends in #d=<design id> (for example inspector.html#d=binder_l68_s123456_mpnn3), so a link opens the same design. Links such as board.html#f=<family id> and map.html#m=M3 do the same for a family and a mode.

Design listWhich design do I look at next?Prev, Next or ← and → step through the list; / focuses the filter box (id, family, run, mode, status or verdict words) and the chips narrow it: Clean, Issues, Fix first, Accepted, Near miss, Undecided. StructureWhat does the pose look like, and where is the evidence?A 3D view: drag to rotate, wheel to zoom, click a residue to fly to it, hover for its details. Pull apart slides the binder away from the target. Click a finding and its residues light up. Without WebGL you get a simplified view, which draws the reference chain as the target.
Findings tabWhat did the checks find, and what would settle it?A list sorted Fix or explain, Worth a look, Context, Looks good. Click one: the 3D view flies to the evidence and a suggested next step opens. Above the list: the Target frame strip and the Target checks (below). For a design on a scaffold the list also says which designed segments touch the target, how many framework residues sit in the interface (numbered as in the scaffold) and, for a VHH, what became of the FR2 face, the exposed face of framework 2 that sat against the light chain in a conventional antibody. A changed face with no contacts is more likely the design making that surface less hydrophobic than part of the paratope.
Tag and fusionWhich end of this design can carry a tag, and what would it cost?A card at the top of the Findings tab. The headline names the end to use for the tag size in force (His6, Twin-Strep, GFP / SNAP, Fc; the buttons switch size, and the choice follows you to the Board). Under it a column per end with its grade — fits as it is, needs a linker of about n residues, or no room, even with a 15-residue linker (or the end itself touches the target) — a chip per tag size, and the evidence: distance to the target, which way the chain leaves the fold, whether the end sits on the paratope side (a tag there holds the binder by its binding face, which matters for SPR and BLI), burial, confidence and the room around it. Hover an end to see it in 3D as a sphere with an arrow for the exit direction; click to fly there. The two unstructured tags are judged by whether a chain of their length can route to open solvent, the two folded fusions by whether a sphere of their size fits in the cone the chain can leave in. A design whose molecule has no free end (a cyclic peptide) says so instead.
Target frame stripWhere does the construct sit in the full-length protein?A strip of the full-length protein: its domains (when the profile lists them), your construct, the stretches this run removed (hatched), the cut ends, the hotspots, the contacted residues, the interface with the other subunit and glycan sites.
Target checksDoes the binder fit on the full-length target?Cards for the other subunit, the rest of the protein (nearest atom, atoms within 3, 4 and 6 Å, residues it would bury), cut ends, glycan distance and membrane side, as far as the data exist. Each card shows its numbers; click it for a suggested next step and what would confirm or refute it, and the 3D view flies to the residues involved.
Interface tabHow is the interface built?A contact diagram (target left, binder right; H-bonds, salt bridges, other contacts), the buried area per residue and the ten largest contacts. For a scaffolded design, Paratope by segment sits above the diagram: one bar per segment with its interface residues (BindCraft's list); click a segment to see its residues in 3D.
Confidence tabWhere is the prediction trustworthy?pLDDT along the chain and the predictors block. A PAE matrix appears only when the data carry one; BindCraft writes none.
Specificity tabWould it bind other species or related proteins?A Reading of the specificity rules (cross-species reactivity and off-target risk, each with the assay that would settle it; it appears only when your profile defines such rules), epitope conservation, a species table with the residues that differ, paralog bars, the residue-by-residue alignment and four stickiness proxies (exposed hydrophobic patch, charge, interface hydrophobicity, aromatic and Arg share). A sequence screen, not a measurement. Needs species and paralog data (see when data are missing).
Siblings tabWhat do the other sequences on this backbone look like?The sequences on the same backbone with their scores and a bar of the positions that differ from the first one. A rejected sequence is listed but never suggested as better.
Sequence tracksWhich residues carry the issue?Per residue: sequence with interface residues underlined, structure, segments (for a scaffolded design), pLDDT, buried area and flags (unpaired Cys, liabilities, sticky patches), then for the target the numbering, hotspots, contacts, conservation and paralog identity. Hover a residue to see it in 3D; click to fly there. Residues the run removed are hatched.
Rejected near-missesWhy did BindCraft reject this one?A near-miss design has a finding that lists every filter it failed, with value and limit. It is decided per design, not per family.

Easy to miss in the Inspector

Two numbers carry the name unsatisfied polars: the meter Unsat. (mean) is BindCraft's own Rosetta count, while the findings, the Siblings tab and the Board use the count Binderscope recomputes from the structure, so they can disagree. 4A 15-minute review recipe

The order that wastes the least of your time. It assumes a bundle with one or two runs and a few dozen families.

  1. Judge the campaign before the designs

    On the Board, read Do you need more designs? for each run. Still discovering means more designs will pay off; Diminishing returns or Saturated means your time is better spent choosing. Treat the estimate as rough below about 30 families. On a scaffolded campaign read What the designs bind with too: fold is not counted there, so coverage describes binding sites only.

    Board · funnel and diversity
  2. Remove what cannot work

    In Query, switch on the rules that matter for your target: touches a hotspot, clear of the other subunit, clear of the rest of the protein. Look at what disappears and why, and open Near-misses to see what a single rule costs you. To mark the survivors on the Board, Send to board as Maybe.

    Query · rule stack
  3. Walk the families

    Back on the Board, step through the tiles with the arrow keys. Press K, M or X, and right after a reject tag a reason with 1 to 7. Keep it quick: families you cannot decide on become maybes.

    Board · tiles
  4. Look hard at the maybes

    Open each in the Inspector. Read the headline, the Target checks, the interface findings and the liabilities under Worth a look. Decide.

    Inspector
  5. Check specificity

    For every keep, open the Specificity tab. If you need cross-species reactivity, read the orthologs; if off-target binding worries you, read the paralogs. This needs species and paralog data (see when data are missing).

    Inspector · Specificity
  6. Spread your bets

    The note under the Order list says how many families, clusters and binding modes your keeps cover. If they pile up in one mode, look at the Map's Quality-diversity picks and swap one or two keeps for picks from other modes.

    Board · order list, then Map · Mode detail
  7. Export and save

    Copy the order list as FASTA or export it as CSV or a FASTA file, and save the review file so you can reload it later.

    Board · order list

5More workflows

Five common jobs, each as a short sequence of steps. They use the same pages as the recipe above.

Choosing designs to order · a competition or a limited lab budget

When lab slots are limited, the question is not "which designs score highest?" but "which set of designs gives me the best chance that at least one of them binds?"

Diversify

Spend slots on different ideas

Pick across families and binding modes. Two designs from one backbone share most of their risk; two from different modes share very little.

Context

Remove what cannot bind

Designs that collide with the part of the target BindCraft never saw, or sit on the wrong face, cost a slot and teach you little.

Hedge

Keep near-misses in play

A design BindCraft rejected by a hair, on the right site, can be a better bet than a polished one on the wrong site.

Tag

Decide the tag with the design, not after it

The end a construct is tagged at is part of the order. Where a tag can go on the Board, and the Tag and fusion card in the Inspector, say which end each design can carry a His6, a Twin-Strep or a larger fusion at, and whether that end is on the binding face; Tag plan CSV takes the answers with you. It is a prediction, so read the evidence before you commit a construct to it.

Record

Write down why

Reasons on every keep and reject, plus the saved review file and the bundle, are the rationale you will want after the results come back.

  1. Fix the budget and the risks

    Write down how many sequences you can order, and which risks matter most to you: the wrong site, a collision with the rest of the protein, cross-species reactivity, off-target binding, sequence liabilities.

    Before you open a page
  2. Screen with rules

    On Query switch on the rules that express those risks (hotspots touched, the context rules, specificity, sequence). Read the funnel for what each group costs, use "kills n alone" to see which rule holds designs back, and open the histogram of a rule to see how narrow the margin is. Send to board the survivors as Maybe.

    Query · rule stack and funnel
  3. Decide per family

    On the Board keep the families you would defend, reject with a reason, leave the rest as maybes. One strong sequence from many families beats several sequences from one.

  4. Check the spread

    Read the note under the Order list: how many families, clusters and binding modes your keeps cover. Open the Map, set Budget to your number of slots and compare the quality-diversity picks with your keeps. Swap keeps that repeat a mode for picks from modes you do not yet cover.

    Add one or two near-misses on the right site, following Rescuing near-misses. They are exported from the Query page, not from the order list.

Rescuing near-misses · designs BindCraft rejected by a hair

A near-miss is a BindCraft-rejected design that missed exactly one of BindCraft's filters by at most 25% of its threshold. BindCraft applies its AlphaFold2 confidence pre-filters before a sequence gets a row, so designs lost there leave no row and no structure and cannot be rescued; near-miss lists contain only failures of the filters that act on scored sequences.

  1. Add the rejected designs

    On Query set Pool to + BindCraft-rejected. Only rejected designs that have a structure are in it; the Overview's Runs table says which runs kept them.

    Query · Pool
  2. Say what you cannot compromise on

    Switch on the rules for the site and the context (hotspots touched, clear of the other subunit and the rest of the protein). Leave the BindCraft filters on: they define what "near" means.

    Query · rule stack
  3. Read the near-misses

    Open Near-misses and tick BindCraft near-misses only. Each row names the rule that blocks it here and the BindCraft filters it missed, with value, limit and gap. A miss on a filter you do not mind (a surface hydrophobicity a hair over) on the right site is the classic rescue; a miss on interface confidence is a bigger risk.

    Query · Near-misses
  4. Look at a few

    Open the best candidates in the Inspector (the Near miss chip lists them) and check the context, the pose and the findings as for any design. The Inspector has no export; K, M or X there record a per-design rescue only.

    Inspector
  5. Rescue and export

    Press Rescue as maybe to record the design with the missed filters as the reason, then CSV or FASTA to take the list away. Rejected designs are not tiles on the Board, so rescued designs do not enter the Board's order list: a dashed line under it counts them and links back. Add them to your submission by hand.

    Query · Rescue as maybe, CSV, FASTA

    On the Overview read the Runs table and the What stands out item about differences: hotspots, the construct as each run saw it, length range and settings.

    Overview
  2. Read each run on its own

    Board · RUNS
  3. Compare where they bind

    On the Map pick a run and look at its hotspots, the residues it lacks (hatched) and the by-run counts in the mode detail. The By mode view shows which modes each run covers.

    Query · rule scope

    A run that is still discovering new clusters pays off with more designs. A saturated run needs different hotspots, lengths or a different construct, not more of the same.

6Reading the checks

A check is a question, not an answer. Here is what each asks and what would settle it.

The Board's verdict comes from these checks. NO PASS when a hard check fails (Fold, Interface, Pose, and Context only when the reference is an experimental structure; see below). REVIEW when a soft check fails (Chemistry, Epitope, Sequence, and Context otherwise) or two or more checks warn (soft checks, Pose and Context all count). Otherwise PASS. A check that was not run, for want of a reference structure, is not a pass: the Board's Context row reads not checked and says why. Specificity never counts. The limits are those of the threshold preset in force; with the default profile that is BindCraft's own default filter set, quoted below. The preset is set in the profile: the Board and the Inspector have no control for it, while the Map's Thresholds and the Query page's preset select apply another preset (Literature-informed, Relaxed, Strict) to their own counts only. If you write your own profile, set its default preset: a profile that names none falls back to the stricter literature-informed set (i_pTM at least 0.70, i_pAE at most 0.30, pTM at least 0.75, three hotspots), under which designs BindCraft accepted can read NO PASS.

A passing design is a predicted binder, not a confirmed one.

In published benchmarks, confidence scores are only weak predictors of lab success on designs that already passed the filters. Use agreement between checks as a flag, never as a gate. The context checks rest on a placement from your reference structure, and the Sequence check rests on sequence patterns, not on measurements.

Things that surprise people

  • A tile shows the verdict of the family's best design (the highest quality score); its sibling sequences may read differently, and the Board counts a family as passing when its best sequence is not NO PASS.
  • A design BindCraft accepted can read NO PASS (these checks are Binderscope's own), and a BindCraft-rejected one can read PASS. Membrane side, glycan distance and specificity never change the verdict, even where they show as amber or red elsewhere.
  • The Interface and Fold checks use BindCraft's own averaged scores. For a BindCraft 1 run the limits in BindCraft's filters are reconstructed defaults, not your filter file (see the FAQ).
  • The Inspector's Promising, with n issues counts every warning finding, so a PASS can come with issues. One warning alone never changes the Board's verdict.
  • If every accepted design fails Context, or they all hit the same residues, suspect the reference before the designs (see the warning under when data are missing).

What the checks need

  • Fold, Interface, Pose, Chemistry, Epitope, Sequence run on every bundle. A metric a run does not report (for example from a BindCraft 2 run) removes the part of the check that needs it, and a missing value never fails a design.
  • Context is not run without a reference structure (see below). With one, it needs the reference structure to contain the other subunits (the build finds them, or you name them with --partner-chains) and the parts of the chain outside your construct. Without other chains there is no other-subunit part; without extra residues in the reference there is no rest-of-the-protein part. The Overview lists which parts are on. Its distances depend on how the design is placed on the reference (see Target frame under concepts) and on how well the reference places the rest of the chain (see the warning under when data are missing).
  • Epitope needs the hotspots of each run (settings JSON or CSV column). A run without hotspots has nothing to test and the check passes; its row then reads 0 of 0 hotspots touched, which is not a result (see when data are missing).
  • Specificity needs species and paralog data (see when data are missing).

i
The reference decides how good the full-length checks are

The checks that use the reference (context, cut ends, buried epitope) are only as good as it is: a model of a different construct, a reference with missing loops or one that lacks the other subunit gives fewer or weaker checks. They are flags that say what to confirm, not measurements.

!
Check how well the reference places the rest of the protein.

An AlphaFold model of a protein with several domains places them relative to each other only as well as its predicted aligned error (PAE) says. Where the PAE between your construct and a stretch is near 30 Å (the scale tops out near 32 Å, where AlphaFold is saying it has no idea), that stretch is placed essentially at random. A clash, cut-end or buried-epitope finding that involves it means nothing in either direction: a design reported as colliding there may not, and one reported clear is not shown clear. This is why Context is advisory unless the reference is experimental: its findings still appear, with their reasons, but cannot give NO PASS on their own. Two signs that the reference is the problem, not the designs: every accepted design fails the rest-of-protein check, and they all hit the same residues. On a real campaign every accepted design was reported as clashing, all on the same residues of a region the model could not place, and every family read NO PASS on that check, which also hid the sequence liabilities that were real; Context is now advisory for a predicted reference, so such designs read REVIEW with the liabilities listed. Binderscope does not read the model's PAE itself, so look at the PAE plot of the model before you rely on a clash, or on a NO PASS that rests on Context (Binderscope's campaign interface, where you launch runs, checks it on the Review tab when you choose the reference). If the reference is poor, build against one that stops at the domain you designed against, or an experimental structure of it. The interface numbers (contacts, hydrogen bonds, buried area) come from the design's own structure and do not depend on the reference.

9Saving, sharing and exporting

Where your decisions live

  • Autosave. Each decision is saved in this browser's local storage under the bundle's id (the same inputs give the same id in any folder), together with the reviewer name and the learning switch. Clearing site data erases it, so save a review file. If the browser blocks storage the page says so and decisions live in memory until you close the tab. In the app a copy is also saved in the project folder (review-events.json) a few seconds after each change and when you leave the page, and brought back when a page opens in a browser that lacks some decisions (another browser, another port, cleared site data): the merge is a union by event id, so nothing is overwritten. Deleting the project deletes the copy.
  • Review file. Save review file writes <reviewer>.jsonl, a small text file with one line per event: a keep, maybe or reject, a reason tag, a clear. Each event carries the reviewer, a time stamp, the family or design id, the run, the sequences chosen for the order list, the reasons, the learning switch and the bundle id. It holds the events of the current reviewer only, and the save button is available as long as the log holds anything, so a file can come out empty if the log holds only a colleague's events. Browsers cannot write into the bundle themselves: move the downloaded file into the bundle's reviews/ folder if you want to keep them together, but note that the pages never read that folder by themselves and that writing a bundle into an existing folder (import --bundle, or build-bundle without --keep; in the app Rebuild and Update the review pages) deletes it, reviews/ included. Keep a second copy elsewhere.
  • Load review file. Reads one back and merges it: events whose id is already present are skipped, new events are added, nothing is overwritten, and a decision in the file that a later Clear decisions removed here is brought back as a new event. Invalid lines are reported with their line number; events written for a different bundle are counted. The current decision on an item is the latest event of that reviewer.
  • Reviewer name. The default is "you". Set your name in the Share dialog before you start; the file is named after it. If two people use the same name their events cannot be told apart, and a typo in the name makes your decisions look empty (the events are still in the log: typing the old name brings them back).
  • Share. Share in the header (also Share review in the order list) opens a dialog with Save and Load review file, the reviewer name, a picker to switch between the reviewers found in the log, and a note on whether decisions persist. The Board shows one reviewer at a time and does not merge their calls. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Lists you take away

  • Order list (Board). Copy FASTA puts the sequences on the clipboard for a submission form. FASTA file and CSV download order-list.fasta and order-list.csv (columns: design id, run, family, rank, verdict, BindCraft rank, reasons). Tag plan CSV downloads tag-plan.csv: one row per design in the list with the end to tag for each of the four tag sizes, what to immobilise through, and the distance, side and room behind each answer. FASTA needs the sequences to be in the bundle; the exporter can leave them out.
  • Query results. CSV (n) and FASTA (n) act on the ticked designs or the whole tab; the CSV never contains a sequence. Save writes a rule-set file (binderscope-rules-<profile id>.json) and Load applies one; the file holds numbers and ids, no sequences and no design ids.
  • Map. Copy FASTA on the quality-diversity picks. The star shortlist is not saved.

What is in a bundle

A bundle is a folder. In the app it is the bundle folder of the project (Binderscope/<project>/bundle in your home folder); on the command line it is the folder you name with --bundle.

10Good practice and pitfalls

Do

Count families

Ten sequences from one backbone are one idea. Spread across families and binding modes.

Do

Write the reason

A reason beside every reject makes the next campaign's rules obvious.

Do

Look at the maybes in 3D

Numbers hide pose problems that take seconds to see.

Do

Save a review file

Browser storage is a convenience, not an archive. Save the file when you stop, and keep a copy outside the bundle folder (a rebuild into the same folder deletes it).

Avoid

Comparing runs by pooled rates

If the hotspots, lengths or target construct changed, it is a different experiment.

Avoid

Trusting one score

Several weak signals that agree are worth more than one strong number.

Avoid

Reading flags as vetoes

Only a failed Fold, Interface or Pose check gives NO PASS (and Context, when the reference is an experimental structure), and even that is a screen: the context checks rest on a placement from the reference structure and on how well it places the rest of the protein.

11FAQ

Does anything leave my computer?

No design data does. The app runs a small server on your own computer (it listens on 127.0.0.1 only, so other computers cannot reach it) and the pages load from it or from files on disk; no binder sequence or design id is sent anywhere. The only network requests are public lookups that you ask for: pressing Download for a PDB id (the id goes to the RCSB) and ticking Allow the online lookup (public records fetched by accession or PDB id at build time; --online on the command line). By default a build reads a local cache and makes no requests; the Overview's Build panel records it. A bundle makes no network request when you open it (it uses the fonts of your system; --fonts google adds an optional typeface link). The learning ranking on the Board runs in your browser. The installer downloads the Python libraries from PyPI once.

Why are some numbers marked "unknown" or "est."?

BindCraft 1 does not record every attempt in the files Binderscope reads, so the number of attempted trajectories reads "unknown", with an estimate from BindCraft's failure counters marked "est." where the counters allow one. A BindCraft 2 campaign lists every trajectory it started, so its number is known. Unknown is never drawn as zero.

Why do the two runs have different targets or hotspots?

Each run is read with its own settings. If one run deleted loops from the construct or used other hotspots, the pages follow that run. The Overview lists the differences, and the Inspector's target frame strip hatches what a run removed.

My run has no hotspots. Can I still use it?

Yes. A run with no hotspots at all (no settings file and an empty hotspot column, or a BindCraft 2 campaign without its metadata file) imports and builds. It has no intended epitope, so the Epitope check passes, no design is off-site and counts such as 0 of 0 hotspots touched mean nothing. The Overview says No hotspots recorded for run01, and the binding-site view is built from the residues the designs contact most. See when data are missing.

Why "PASS · Promising, with 2 issues" in the Inspector?

The first word is the Board's verdict. The second part is the Inspector's finer reading: it counts every warning finding, such as a Met oxidation or a contact near a cut end, while the Board leaves PASS only after a failed soft check or two warnings. Read the findings, not the label.

My decisions disappeared.

They live in the browser's local storage under the bundle's id and the page's address. In the app a copy is also kept in the project folder and brought back when the page opens, so another browser, another port or cleared site data does not lose them. A bundle built from other inputs, a project you deleted, or a bundle opened from disk (it has another address) starts fresh: load your saved review file. In Firefox the store for a page opened from disk may be kept separately for each page, so use the app, Chrome or Edge, the browsers the pages are tested in. Clear decisions asks first; U restores the cleared decisions at once, and loading a review file you saved earlier brings them back as new events.

Can I add a run later?

Open the project's More menu and choose Edit the inputs…: the import page opens with the project's choices filled in. Add the run and press Build project. The app makes a new project (named like the old one with " 2" added) and leaves the old one as it is. The bundle id changes with the inputs, so load your review file in the new project to carry decisions over (they refer to family and design ids, which stay the same); delete the old project when you no longer need it. On the command line, build again with all the run folders, into a new bundle folder or after copying your review files out of the old one (a rebuild into the same folder replaces its contents). The Board's How to import a run button only explains the command and lists what the build read; it does not import anything itself.

Why do the BindCraft filters not match the ones I ran with?

BindCraft 1 does not copy its filter file into the run folder. For a BindCraft 1 run Binderscope attaches BindCraft's default filter set, reconstructed from the data, and says how firmly each limit is known (counter-verified, bounded or memory). If you ran custom filters, the BindCraft filters group on the Query page and the limits on the Inspector's meters are not yours: edit the limits there, or write them into your profile. BindCraft 2 does record its thresholds in campaign_metadata.json, so a BindCraft 2 campaign is judged by the thresholds it used (each rule's note says it is the limit the campaign recorded, and so does the tooltip of the Inspector's meters): that is a stronger claim than a reconstruction, but still a screen, not a verdict. A design BindCraft rejected stays rejected even when its CSV values pass a threshold (BindCraft checks confidence filters on each validation model, the CSV holds the mean), and thresholds on metrics Binderscope does not carry are listed as not re-checked.

The binder looks misplaced on the full-length protein. What do I check?

Start with the chain. The import page says how your target structure fits the chain of the reference (green, amber or red, with the CA RMSD and the residues matched); red means the wrong chain, another protein or another numbering. Set Chain of the target in the reference in Options (for an existing project, More and Edit the inputs…) and build again. Then the protein: the reference must be the full-length version of the same protein. Then How designs are placed on the full-length protein in Options: the default fits around each design's interface, while the whole-target fit can leave the binder 1 to 3 Å off where BindCraft's copy of the target differs from your reference (a project whose card says "Built with an older analysis" used it: press Rebuild). Then the numbers: in the Inspector's Target checks a design fails the other-subunit or the rest-of-protein check by default from 10 atoms within 3 Å, and if nearly every design of a run does, suspect the placement, then the reference (see the warning under when data are missing), before the designs. Last, open structures/<design id>.pdb of the bundle in a text editor: the line REMARK 250 placement: gives the fit used and the CA RMSD near the interface and over the whole target, and more than 1.5 Å near the interface means that the design's own copy of the target differs from the reference there (the build records a warning for it). On the command line the option is --placement interface|whole|core. See the app guide.

Why does a design BindCraft accepted read NO PASS?

The Board's verdict is Binderscope's own set of checks, not BindCraft's decision. The usual reasons are a Fold, Interface or Pose limit in your profile that is stricter than BindCraft's, or a Context failure against an experimental reference (binder atoms within 3 Å of the other subunit or of the part of the protein BindCraft never saw); against a predicted reference a Context failure only adds a reason to a REVIEW. The Board's because list ends with a line saying BindCraft accepted the design and that the verdict is Binderscope's own screen. Open the design: the because list and the Target checks say which. If every accepted design fails Context on the same residues, suspect the reference before the designs (see the warning under when data are missing).

How do I change the limits behind PASS, REVIEW and NO PASS?

In your profile (checks.presets and checks.preset_default, and checks.context for the context limits). See the profile specification. The Board and the Inspector have no control for it.

Where are my projects, and how do I update or remove Binderscope?

Projects are folders in Binderscope in your home folder (More, then Show in Finder, opens one). To update, run the installer again (it asks a running Binderscope to quit); projects built by an older version say so on the home page, and Update the review pages brings them up to date in seconds. A card that says "Built with an older analysis" needs Rebuild instead: the update changed how designs are placed on the full-length protein, and new pages alone do not recompute that. To remove it, run ./install.sh --uninstall: it removes the app and keeps your projects. The app guide has the details.

The window says "Binderscope has stopped".

The app ended: you pressed Quit, or about a minute passed with no open window and no build running. Open Binderscope again from Spotlight. Your projects and decisions are unchanged.

Can I use BindCraft 2 output?

Yes. The reader was verified against a real de novo campaign (the binder modality) written by BindCraft 2 v1.0.3, and a campaign of that version and modality with its metadata file is read without a warning. Every other BindCraft 2 campaign is read on the same assumptions but is not verified: another version, a VHH, scFv, Fab or cyclic-peptide campaign, several targets, a missing metadata file, or tables that lack a column the reader relies on. The Overview marks such a run "experimental reader" and the Build panel's Warnings row says why, so compare a few numbers with your own CSVs. Checks that need scores BindCraft 2 does not report are switched off. A campaign that numbers its binder residues from 0 is analysed through renumbered copies of its structure files (your files are not changed). See when data are missing.
